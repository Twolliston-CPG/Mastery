### 2. The funnel diagnosis
Where does it stop? What is the **one variable** you will change in weeks 27+?

### 3. Rejection reasons
List every one you learned. What is the pattern? What does it tell you to study?

### 4. The five best "Surprised" lines
From the incident journal, across all 26 weeks. These are your best stories — make sure each one is
in the STAR bank.

### 5. What you'd do differently
If you started again on week 1, what would you change? (This is also a good interview answer about
self-direction.)

### 6. Sustainability
Rest days taken, out of 26. If the number is low, that is the finding — the search continues for
months, and a burned-out candidate interviews badly.

## The weeks 27+ plan — write it down

A search without a written cadence drifts, and drift turns a three-month search into a nine-month
one.

```markdown
# Weeks 27+ cadence

## Weekly, every week, until hired
- [ ] 20-25 quality applications          (8h)
- [ ] 5 networking conversations, 1 event (4h)
- [ ] Interview prep, per company         (6h)
- [ ] Build: <the specific thing>         (10h)
- [ ] One blog post                       (3h)
- [ ] CKA prep, if not done               (8h)
- [ ] ONE FULL REST DAY                   (non-negotiable)

## The one variable I am changing
<from the funnel diagnosis>

## Review date
Week 34. If 100+ quality applications have produced no technical screens by then,
the problem is upstream of skill. Change the résumé, or the targeting, or go
all-in on referrals for a month. Do not respond to no-response by sending more
of the same application.

## Certification
CKA by: ______    (the K8s cert that actually moves hiring decisions)

## The fallback ladder, if month 9 arrives with no offer
1. Cloud support engineering -- highest volume, converts internally
2. Contract / contract-to-hire -- faster process, converts often
3. Consultancies and MSPs directly, not via job boards
4. Internal IT / NOC at a company with a platform team, then transfer
```

---

## What to remember

Week 26 is not the finish line. It is the point where the pipeline is full and converting, and the
remaining variable is **time and volume, not capability**.

You have ~1,000 focused hours, three shipped systems, ~50 diagnosed incidents, a dozen published
posts, and a demonstrated ability to learn something hard, fast — which is the thing employers are
actually buying at this level.

**Keep building. Keep applying. Keep talking to people. The offer arrives.**
